# Prophecies of Religious and Institutional Upheaval

Another major theme in Cannon’s work – especially via her Conversations with Nostradamus series – is the prophesied collapse or transformation of powerful religious and institutional structures. Cannon reported that Nostradamus, speaking through her hypnotized subjects, warned of tumult in the Catholic Church, including the reign of a final Pope and the downfall of the papacy 15 16 . According to Cannon’s dialogues with Nostradamus, the last three popes would fall in quick succession, with an assassination, a short-lived pontificate, and a corrupt final pope allied with the forces of evil (the Antichrist) leading to the Church’s demise 15 16 . As one excerpt puts it: “The present pope will be assassinated and the next pope will not last long. The final pope will be a tool of the Antichrist…The last three popes of the Catholic Church will fall in short succession…The last, the one born slightly misshapen, will go the greatest distance in destroying the church…his death will signal the end of the Catholic Church.” 15 16 . This dramatic prophecy mirrored, in some ways, the medieval Prophecy of St. Malachy which also predicts a final Pope presiding over the Church’s tribulations 17 18 . Cannon’s Nostradamus went so far as to cheerfully anticipate the assassination of Pope John Paul II in

2 the 1990s (believing a quatrain about “when the roses bloom” signaled a springtime killing) 19 20 . In 1996, Cannon was quoted acknowledging, “We’ve been waiting for the present pope to be assassinated… it didn’t happen… our calculations were off, not because the prophecies are wrong,” underscoring her conviction in the foretold Vatican upheaval 19 20 .

Historical outcomes thus far: Pope John Paul II was not assassinated (though an attempt in 1981 nearly fulfilled that prophecy). He died in 2005 of natural causes, somewhat beyond Nostradamus’s indicated timeline. However, subsequent events lend an intriguing if partial parallel to Cannon’s predictions. The “next pope will not last long” in her account was Pope Benedict XVI, who indeed had a relatively short reign (2005– 2013) and took the highly unusual step of resignation 21 22 . His abdication – the first papal resignation in nearly 600 years – shocked the institution and could be seen as a destabilizing moment for the Church, consistent with the idea of papal succession turmoil. The final pope in prophecy would then be Pope Francis (elected 2013). Cannon’s sources described this last pope as a “tool of the Antichrist” who would undermine the Church from within 15 . While it would be extreme to label Pope Francis such a figure, it is true that his papacy has been marked by significant internal conflict and calls for change. Francis has introduced reforms and a more progressive tone that traditionalists in the Church fiercely resist, leading to open rifts. Notably, some ultraconservative Catholic circles even accuse Pope Francis of heresy or view him as a false pope – a narrative oddly resonant with Nostradamus’s predicted “treachery of the final pope” 23 24 . Furthermore, Church attendance and influence, especially in the Western world, have continued to

decline in Francis’s era, and massive scandals (such as decades of clergy abuse coming to light) have eroded the Church’s moral authority. In that sense, the “downfall of the Catholic Church” that Cannon said was foreseen might be interpreted not as a sudden collapse by 2000 (as she originally expected 25 ), but as a gradual implosion of credibility and power in the early 21st century.

Beyond Catholicism, Cannon’s works (and Nostradamus’s quatrains) hinted at a broader collapse of institutional religion and even secular institutions. For example, Nostradamus allegedly predicted a time when traditional religious structures would give way to a new spiritual understanding after much turmoil 26  27 . Cannon’s Convoluted Universe material also speaks of the old systems (political, economic, religious)

breaking down as humanity shifts to a new age. We see real-world signs of this institutional erosion today: public trust in governments, media, and even science establishments has been faltering in many countries, sometimes fueled by scandals or the rapid pace of change. Specifically focusing on religion, the trend in many regions is toward secularization or personalized spirituality. For instance, in Europe and North America, organized churches (Catholic and Protestant alike) have seen membership and influence wane significantly in the past few decades. Meanwhile, alternative spiritual movements and non-institutional belief systems (meditation groups, New Age philosophies, etc.) have grown – suggesting a transformation in how people find meaning.

Current developments at the Vatican provide some striking correlations to Cannon’s prophecy narrative. In 2023, Pope Benedict XVI passed away (nearly a decade after his resignation), leaving Pope Francis as the sole living pontiff – an unprecedented situation of a “final” pope without a predecessor. Pope Francis’s health and age raise questions about succession, and there is heightened speculation that whoever follows him will inherit a deeply divided Church, possibly fulfilling a form of institutional crisis. Additionally, Pope Francis’s reign has coincided with the Vatican gradually releasing once-secret files (e.g. opening archives on WWII-era Pope Pius XII) and speaking more openly about topics like the possibility of extraterrestrial life (the Vatican Observatory’s chief has said ET belief wouldn’t contradict faith). In Conversations with Nostradamus, it was suggested that after the final Pope, secrets kept by the Church would be exposed 28 29 . One interpretation is that we live in that unfolding now – as transparency and scandal expose shake

3 the Church, many hidden truths (about abuse, historical misdeeds, etc.) have indeed come out. The Catholic Church is being forced into either transformation or decline. As one commentator noted during the height of abuse revelations, “recent headlines from the Vatican make you wonder what’s really happening under the groin vaults” 30 31 – implying a crisis of integrity that Nostradamus/Cannon foresaw.

Looking toward the near future, will we see the full “collapse” of the Catholic Church or a major religious upheaval? While a literal abolition of the papacy is unlikely imminently, experts acknowledge that the Church is at a crossroads: it could undergo drastic reform (e.g. married priests, female roles, financial overhauls) or risk a schism and further loss of followers. Interestingly, the so-called “Prophecy of the Popes” attributed to St. Malachy also names the current Pope as the last before Rome’s destruction 17 18 . Cannon was aware of these kinds of prophecies and believed they were converging in our era. If another major shock hits the Vatican (such as a sudden papal death or a new scandal), public faith might plummet to the point that organized Catholicism as we know it significantly diminishes. On the other hand, Cannon’s Nostradamus emphasized that humanity could alter these outcomes. In her narratives, prophecy served as a warning, not an unchangeable fate 32 2 .

Patterns and credibility: We observe a pattern of partial fulfillment – Cannon’s predictions about papal turmoil were not exactly realized in detail (timing of assassinations, etc.), yet the spirit of those predictions aligns with real stress fractures in the Catholic Church. Moreover, in a larger sense, institutional authority is undergoing a shake-up worldwide, whether through political revolutions, public demand for transparency, or the rise of decentralized networks (like social media movements that challenge traditional gatekeepers). Cannon’s work often spoke of old systems breaking to make way for the new, which resonates with what we see today. Many institutions formed in previous centuries are struggling to adapt to 21st-century challenges, and some are indeed “collapsing” (for example, authoritarian regimes being toppled, or even the disintegration of long-standing nation unions, etc.).

In conclusion, Cannon’s prophecies of religious/institutional upheaval have a mixed track record but notable overlaps with reality. The Catholic Church has not fallen, but it is undeniably in a vulnerable, transformative state – arguably the most profound crisis of confidence it has faced in modern history, consistent with prophecy imagery 25 33 . Other institutions too are facing unprecedented scrutiny. These elements of her work highlight that the coming true of a prophecy may not always be literal, but the essence (loss of trust in old authorities) should not be ignored. If humanity is indeed in a transition period as she suggested, understanding and preparing for major institutional shifts is crucial. Nostradamus, through Cannon, urged people to be alert and proactive in the face of these changes, rather than complacent 34 35 – advice that seems quite pertinent as we navigate upheavals in our religious and political landscapes today.
