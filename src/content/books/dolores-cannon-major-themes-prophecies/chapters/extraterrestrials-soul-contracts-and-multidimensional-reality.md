# Extraterrestrials, Soul Contracts, and Multidimensional Reality

Beyond prophecy and spirituality, Dolores Cannon extensively explored topics like extraterrestrial life, reincarnation, and the existence of multiple dimensions. These themes weave through books such as Keepers of the Garden and The Custodians (focused on ET contacts), Between Death and Life (the afterlife and soul planning), and numerous case studies within Convoluted Universe. Cannon’s body of work suggests that humanity is far from alone in the universe and that our souls partake in a much grander, multi-dimensional journey than one Earth life.

11 Extraterrestrial contact and cosmic citizenship: Cannon claimed, based on hypnosis sessions, that extraterrestrial beings have been interacting with Earth for millennia. In Keepers of the Garden, a subject under regression recounted that he had a past life as an extraterrestrial who helped seed life on Earth – portraying Earth as a sort of cosmic “garden” tended by advanced ET gardeners. Cannon asserted that many of the individuals she regressed uncovered memories of encounters aboard spacecraft or even lives on other planets 74 75 . In The Custodians, she compiled numerous cases of people who described being abducted or visited by aliens, but often with a benevolent or research-oriented twist (e.g. that these beings are monitoring human development or even uplifting our genetics). She spoke of various alien species – some human-like, some energy-based – involved in Earth’s evolution. A recurring point was that the ETs are benevolent overseers concerned about our potential self-destruction (through nuclear war or environmental ruin) and thus subtly guiding us. This ties back into the “volunteer souls” concept: Cannon believed many souls incarnating as humans now actually originate from other star systems or higher dimensions, having come to assist Earth from the inside 76 77 .

Her material also alluded to secret knowledge and cover-ups about ETs. For instance, Nostradamus apparently mentioned “dark secrets of our times” including “secret military weapon researches” and a hidden cabal manipulating world events 78 79 – which in modern conspiracy lore often includes suppressed knowledge of UFOs. Cannon’s interviews in the 1990s came amid a rise in UFO awareness (the X-Files era). She believed the truth about extraterrestrial contact would eventually come out, possibly as part of the great shift.

Soul contracts and reincarnation: Between Death and Life and many session transcripts delve into what happens between incarnations. Cannon’s subjects consistently described a spiritual realm where souls review their life lessons (often called the life review), then plan their next life in accordance with karma and desired growth. This involves soul contracts – agreements made before birth to undergo certain experiences, meet certain people, and even suffer certain trials, all for the purpose of learning. For example, a soul might contract to be part of a difficult relationship in life so both parties can develop forgiveness or compassion. According to Cannon, none of us is here by accident; even major events like the time and manner of one’s death can be part of a pre-birth plan (though there is flexibility and multiple exit points). This view aligns with other metaphysical teachings and was strikingly consistent across her thousands of hypnotherapy sessions. It suggests a universe where reincarnation is real and each life is but one chapter in a long continuum of the soul’s evolution.

She also encountered cases of past-life memories under hypnosis that could be verified to some degree. One famous early case she wrote about was A Soul Remembers Hiroshima, where a subject recalled perishing in the 1945 atomic bombing of Hiroshima – providing details of that life. The prevalence of such accounts in her work contributed to her certainty that reincarnation is factual and that understanding our past lives can heal and enlighten us.

Multidimensional reality: Cannon’s conversations with subjects often ventured into realms beyond the physical. People described experiences on the astral plane, interactions with spirit guides or higher selves, and even parallel lives. In Convoluted Universe, some sessions suggested that time is not linear – a concept that Nostradamus himself reinforced via Cannon by saying “time is fluid and all moments exist simultaneously”, with parallel universes branching out 80 81 . This remarkably mirrors modern multiverse theory in physics, where every choice might spawn an alternate timeline. Cannon asserted that humans have multidimensional aspects – portions of our soul experiencing other dimensions or timelines, usually

12 unknown to our conscious mind. The idea of parallel lifetimes (living out different possibilities in different universes) emerges in her later books, stretching the reader’s imagination of reality.

She also delved into phenomena like teleportation, levitation, and other “miracles,” suggesting these are just poorly understood science that the Great Genius of the future would demystify 82 83 . In Nostradamus’s future vision via Cannon, people eventually develop what we’d call “superhuman” abilities – mental powers unlocked, technology that bridges spiritual and material (like machines that amplify psychic ability). All this resides in a multidimensional framework where mind and matter interplay.

Cross-reference with real-world data and research:

• Extraterrestrials: Public and scientific attitudes toward extraterrestrial life have significantly shifted in recent years. The discovery of thousands of exoplanets (planets around other stars) since the 1990s has made it almost certain that many Earth-like worlds exist. In 2020, scientists even found potential signs of life (phosphine gas) in the clouds of Venus and continue to explore Mars and icy moons for microbial life. While this is about simple life, the possibility of advanced alien civilizations is taken seriously by initiatives like SETI (Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence), which has been listening for signals. So far, no confirmed contact has occurred, but the sheer scale of the universe leads many astronomers to believe we are likely not alone.

More dramatically, the topic of UFOs (now often called UAPs, Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena) has moved from fringe to mainstream government discussion. In 2017, it was revealed that the U.S. Pentagon had a program to study UAPs, and declassified Navy videos showed objects performing extraordinary maneuvers. By 2021, the U.S. Director of National Intelligence released a report acknowledging dozens of unexplained UAP incidents. In July 2023, Congress held a public hearing where former military personnel testified about UAPs. Notably, a former intelligence officer, David Grusch, claimed under oath that the U.S. government possesses recovered “non-human” craft and “biologics” (non-human bodies) from crash sites 84 85 . He spoke of a secret longstanding program to reverse-engineer alien technology 84 . While these

claims are not verified proof, they are astonishingly in line with what Cannon’s more conspiracy-leaning material implied – that authorities have evidence of ET visitation kept hidden. The fact that such statements are being discussed in Congress with some legitimacy suggests the stigma is dropping and perhaps revelations are on the horizon. Some researchers even involve the Vatican archives in this context, speculating that the Church might hold records of historical UFO sightings or alien contact 86 87 . As the History Channel article noted, there’s “wacky speculation” the Vatican hides ETs inside 10 ; Cannon likely would have smiled at that, given her conviction that extraterrestrial knowledge has been suppressed by elites.

The credibility of ET contact as a theme in her work has thus increased: what once sounded like science fiction (government recovering alien craft) is now testified by credible individuals 84 . Even if final proof remains forthcoming, we must acknowledge that humanity could be on the verge of confirming intelligent alien life. The implications would validate Cannon’s portrayals of a larger cosmic community interacting with us. It would also mean her advice to broaden our minds to the cosmos was prescient. Indeed, mainstream voices like astrophysicist Avi Loeb are now openly searching for evidence of alien technology (Loeb hypothesizes objects like the interstellar asteroid ‘Oumuamua might be artificial probes). As we look

13 forward, the potential disclosure of extraterrestrial presence is a major “on the horizon” event. Cannon’s work suggests it might be a positive, consciousness-expanding revelation rather than purely a threat.

• Reincarnation and soul plans: Interestingly, there is a body of research lending some support to reincarnation. At the University of Virginia Division of Perceptual Studies, scientists like Dr. Ian Stevenson and Dr. Jim Tucker have, for decades, studied young children who claim to remember past lives. They’ve documented over 2,200 such cases worldwide where children as young as 2 or 3 provide specific, accurate details about a deceased person’s life, which upon investigation prove true 88  89 . These are often cases with no normal way for the child to know the information (sometimes

in entirely different locales or families). For example, a well-known American case is James Leininger, who from toddler age spoke of being a World War II pilot shot down – later identified to match a real pilot’s life and death 90 91 . Such cases, while not “proof” to all scientists, strongly suggest something beyond our conventional understanding – either reincarnation or some form of genetic or collective memory. The UVA researchers remain open to the survival of consciousness hypothesis. They’ve noted patterns: the children’s memories often fade by age 6-7 (as if the “veil” reasserts itself), and many have birthmarks or phobias related to their past-life death. This empirical research provides a factual backbone to Cannon’s assertions that souls return and carry past impressions. It’s remarkable that an academic database confirms over two thousand cases of children with past-life recollections 88 – a number that legitimizes exploring reincarnation scientifically.

As for soul contracts and life-between-life, that field has been popularized by psychologists like Dr. Michael Newton (who did similar hypnosis work focused on the between-life state). Many of Newton’s clients independently reported choosing their next life circumstances in a council of elders, very similar to Cannon’s findings. While this can’t be as directly verified as past-life memories can, the consistency across subjects who don’t know each other is compelling. Moreover, the concept of a purposeful life resonates with increasing public sentiment that events may have spiritual reasons. Even in therapeutic settings, techniques like past-life regression therapy (which Cannon practiced) have helped individuals overcome phobias or personal issues when they find a past-life cause. The growing acceptance of therapies addressing the soul and karma indicates a shift in how we view mental health – acknowledging perhaps the multidimensional aspect of the person (mind, body, spirit continuum).

• Multidimensional reality: Science has not found direct evidence of parallel universes or extra dimensions at human-scale, but theories abound. The multiverse theory in cosmology posits that our universe might be one bubble among countless others, potentially with different versions of ourselves in them. Quantum physics’ Many-Worlds interpretation explicitly holds that every quantum event spawns new branches of reality – a serious idea in physics since Hugh Everett proposed it. In recent news, scientists have tried to test for mirror universes or higher dimensions (sometimes misreported as “NASA found a parallel universe where time runs backwards” – which was a misconstrued story about anomalous particles in Antarctica 92 93 ). The fact remains that, as a Space.com science article states: “There are now some scientific theories that support the idea of parallel universes beyond our own,” though it remains controversial 67 . Essentially, the scientific community acknowledges that our three-dimensional perception may not be all there is. Experiments in particle physics (like at the LHC) continue to look for signs of extra spatial dimensions. And the field of consciousness studies entertains notions that consciousness might operate nonlocally or in other dimensions (for instance, some hypotheses around quantum processes in the brain by Roger Penrose and Stuart Hameroff hint at consciousness ties to the quantum realm).

14 The intersection of science and Cannon’s multidimensional claims is still largely theoretical, but trending toward openness. If one day a parallel universe interaction is observed or we learn to communicate across dimensions, Cannon’s accounts of subjects who visited parallel lives under hypnosis will take on new significance.

Patterns and credibility: The overarching pattern in these “fringe” topics of Cannon’s work is an expansion of humanity’s perceived reality – outward to space and other beings, inward to the soul’s continuity, and conceptually beyond the limits of linear time and singular reality. Step by step, mainstream thought is catching up: we now talk about habitability of exoplanets regularly, we see serious scientists discussing the likelihood of alien technology (e.g., the Harvard professor’s studies of interstellar objects), we have academic research hinting at reincarnation, and even the U.S. military openly acknowledging mystifying aerial craft. Each of these was once ridiculed or ignored, yet now demands attention. Cannon’s contributions anticipated this broadening of horizons.

Her portrayal of a universe teeming with life and guided by spiritual laws finds echoes in today’s interdisciplinary studies – from astrobiology to parapsychology. It encourages a holistic view: that perhaps spiritual truths (like “we are eternal souls” or “we are not alone in the cosmos”) will eventually be validated as knowledge, not merely belief.

As elements of her work come true (e.g., higher acceptance of UFO reality, serious past-life research) and others unfold (maybe soon discovering microbial life on another planet, or further publicized UFO evidence), the potential credibility of the remaining claims (like open ET contact or conscious interdimensional travel) increases.

Insights not to ignore: Cannon’s insistence on the interconnectedness of these themes is itself an insight – that our evolution involves both the material and spiritual, and even the extraterrestrial. She would argue that acknowledging our soul nature (reincarnation) should make us less afraid of death and thus less prone to hate or fear; acknowledging extraterrestrials could humble and unite us as “Earth humanity” rather than separate nations; understanding multiple dimensions could unleash innovation and cure our existential angst by showing life’s bigger picture. Modern society tends to silo science, spirituality, and history, but Cannon’s work is a synthesis pointing to an integrated understanding of existence.

One clear “take-home” is the importance of an open mind. The UFO example is illustrative: decades of denial, and suddenly we’re told “actually, there are things we can’t explain flying around.” If we ignore evidence due to dogma or fear of ridicule, we stall progress. Cannon was often dismissed by skeptics, yet many points she made have gained traction later. So even if not every claim is literally true, exploring them can yield value. For instance, even if one doesn’t believe in soul contracts, the idea encourages personal responsibility and finding meaning in hardships – a psychologically beneficial perspective. If one is unsure about aliens, simply considering humanity as part of a larger galactic community could inspire cooperation and long-term thinking (as the famous “Overview Effect” does for astronauts who view Earth from space, realizing borders are trivial).

Looking ahead, some specific things to watch (horizon scanning based on these themes) include: official disclosure or contact with extraterrestrial intelligence, which would be a paradigm shift like no other; growing evidence of consciousness beyond the brain, perhaps via advancements in neuroscience or verified accounts of near-death consciousness, which might push science to accept life after death theories; and technological breakthroughs that blur dimensions, such as quantum computing or space-time

15 manipulation, making what once seemed paranormal into normal science. Cannon predicted that eventually science and spirituality would converge – a theme Nostradamus echoed with the Great Genius unifying them 27 26 . Interestingly, today’s frontier science (like consciousness studies, and efforts to reconcile quantum mechanics with relativity) is headed in that direction, seeking a unified understanding of reality.

In conclusion, Dolores Cannon’s explorations of ETs, soul journeys, and multiple realities, when cross- referenced with modern data, show a surprising number of alignments. They encourage us not to dismiss such possibilities outright. Instead, we should foster research and dialogue in these areas. The potential payoff is huge: confirming intelligent alien life or the persistence of the soul would revolutionize human self-concept. Cannon believed humanity was ready (or soon would be) for these revelations. Many signs indicate she might be right. Thus, her counsel to approach these mysteries with curiosity rather than fear remains highly relevant. The more we learn about our cosmic and spiritual environment, the more prepared we will be for whatever comes – be it meeting our “space neighbors” or understanding our true multidimensional nature.
