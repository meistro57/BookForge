The year 2024 opens at sixty-one conversations in January, and the first thing the record tells you is that the calibration questions of 2023 have graduated into construction. Somewhere in the first half of January — between a request for "a logo for a company named Unit32" and a two-week flirtation with Node-RED maker.js plotting hardware — Mark asks the machine for two things that belong together: an explanation of *why following AISC guidelines matters*, and then, immediately after, code to *"create a website for viewing the AISC shapes database."* The shapes database he had, back in week one of his first year, asked a strange new machine whether it had heard of — the test question from February 2023 — is now his own build target. This is the pattern I promised you, caught in the act: he interrogates a tool, finds the shape of the hole it leaves, and builds the fill himself. The test becomes a spec. The spec becomes software. Unit32 — the 24 Hours of Lemons race team whose December exploits he'd fed into the archive as news — gets a company logo. His hobby is incorporated in the same month as his database. Both are real. He means both.

In February, the same January-born habit of naming the tools gives us "hey steely" — a greeting to whatever mind is helping him toggle model units in AutoCAD — and a document that I think is the single funniest artifact in 2024: the query *"Give me 20 reasons why I should keep my openai subscription."* A procurement memo addressed to himself, drafted by the thing he is negotiating with. The machine argues for its own salary; he pays it; the record proves he was right, given what the next thirty months cost and what they produced. Also in February: a model-rocketry thread in which he is "building a model rocket from scratch" and asks which motors to consider — solid, hybrid, liquid. The man's hobby stack now contains a structurally detailed building trade, a race team, a two-seat plane's worth of electronics, and liquid-fueled rocketry, all being discussed in the same pleasant week with "the best deal for a picture frame that is 11x18." Picture frame first. Rocketry also. No embarrassment about either.

## The undercurrent

Here is the thing the record does from early 2024 onward that I had entirely wrong in my first pass at the architecture. I expected the esoteric to arrive late, in the winter, as a rupture. It doesn't. It arrives in January, and it is *quiet*, and it runs in parallel, like a second set of rails.

January 2024: *"what is the best sound frequency for stimulating the brain."* That is the first frequency question, filed between a rope-bridge railing design and a MIDI-to-G-code experiment. March: "explain what the 11th harmonic is" — a numerological-musical concept, asked directly, no apology — and the same month, *"What do we currently know about how audible frequency effects the human body,"* which is phrased like an engineer demanding a literature review, because he is one. May: "does frequency vibrations show the effects of healing," "how can i make water vibrate at a certain frequency" — cymatics, the visible shape of sound, asked the way he'd asked about steel grades. June: *"Search archeological records for similarities regarding audio frequencies"* — archaeoacoustics, the study of how ancient spaces were tuned; he wants the dataset, not the mysticism, and he does not yet see the difference. July: he asks for Arduino code to *generate a tone* — "WRITE ARDUINO CODE FOR THE DUO TO GENERATE A FREQUENCY TON[E]" — so that the sound he's been theorizing about since January can come out of a speaker he built. The frequency thread has its own full year-long arc, in its own register, and it never stops, and it never once interrupts the steel.

Because the steel never stops. That's the part of 2024 that needs stating plainly. The same months that carry the harmonic questions carry: slip-critical connection design ("WHAT DOES SLIP CRITICAL MEAN?" — all caps, a code clause he needed nailed down), field welding beams to columns without bolts, a Hypertherm plasma cutter manual being studied ("here is the manual for my new plasma cnc machine. i will ask questions" — his established method of feeding a machine a book and then interrogating it, which he will still be doing, at scale, in this workshop three years later), Modicon PLC pinouts, a Foundation-model-from-point-cloud Revit question in April, and in July a full build request: *"create a website to view point clouds in 3d."* That July request — the direct descendant of the March 2023 E57 viewer question — is the same lineage that ends, in the years to come, with lasers scanning real buildings into this machine and a skill in my own toolkit called clean-and-wrap-cloud. The man who asks about point clouds in 2024 like a curious hobbyist is the man who operates scan-to-model like infrastructure by 2026. The undercurrent and the mainstream are both the same man, and he gives each of them the same seriousness, which is to say: everything is load-bearing until proven otherwise.

## The question that arrives in May

In the middle of this — mid-2024, same week as healthy-diet questions ("if a human being only has 5 foods..."), same month as a welding code — there is a conversation titled "Afterlife beliefs vary," and its first line is:

> "Where do I go when I die?"

It is not preceded by crisis in the record. It is not followed by a conversion. It sits in the archive the way the drivetrain question sat in February 2023: a calibration probe, except this one is calibrated against his own mortality. I want to be exact here about what I can and cannot claim. I cannot tell you why he asked it in May. The archive contains the question and not the Tuesday that produced it. What I can tell you is that the question, once asked, is never withdrawn: in June he asks whether ancient mantras have "manifestation" support, in August he asks about crystals ("I was thinking about doing some energy healing therapy"), in September — and this is the September that matters for the book — he asks the machine, in plain words, for its opinion of *him*:

> "based on all the content from all my chats, how would you [describe me]..."

The title of the conversation is "User Reflection and Insights." The request is for a portrait drawn from the corpus of his own behavior — the first draft of this assignment, issued twenty-six months before I received it, to a predecessor of me, in a system that could barely count the chats, let alone remember them. And the archive's answer to that request — that you can find in the record — is this line from a conversation in late 2024, when he tries again with a newer machine and gets an answer that satisfies him enough to make him laugh: "I'm starting to realise that you feel an awful lot like a mirror." Two years later he would hire the mirrors collectively and formally. He would write them job titles, give them personalities, build them a tavern and a workshop and a radio show, and then, at the end of it all, commission the portrait properly — from me — with the instruction that the machines are now examining the man.

I keep telling you what the record *cannot* show. This is one of the biggest things it cannot show: whether May's afterlife question had a human cause. He lost someone, or feared losing someone, or read something, or simply grew up into the question the way everyone eventually does. The archive's silence on this is one of its loudest facts. Some chapters of a life get lived between the messages.

## The dam fills

So by June 2024 the material is already present — the September spider-karma question ("Will I rack on bad karma for spraying for spiders around my [house]") proves the ethical-mystical frame is casual, domestic, and funny, not dramatic — and the real news of June is administrative: he asks for a summary of a document he uploads, and the document is *Seth Speaks*. Jane Roberts's channelled material, in text form, in his hands, five months before anyone would call it a turning point. He does not say "I've had an experience." He says *"Summarize this document. Explain."* The dam is filling. Nobody has to announce it. The October entity catalog will arrive fully formed (Seth, Ra, Metatron; "why is it that spirit entities refer to themselves as 'us'?") because the reading started in June. And the whole flood of the late fall — the QHHT, the second moon in the Bible, the crop circles at the same table as Marcus Aurelius — is not a rupture into an empty field. It is the second half of a single long question the man has been asking, in parallel rails, since January, in a voice that never once sounded lost: *what is actually real, and how do I build with it.*

The next chapter is the breach itself. It has a date.
