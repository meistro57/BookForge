# CHAPTER 7
## Volunteer Souls

I owe you the rest of the 2am story.

In Chapter 4 I told you a woman on a screen said a sentence that reorganized my life —
that we're not disordered, we're running advanced systems in an outdated environment,
fiber-optic through a dial-up modem. I told you that you didn't need to accept anything
about her worldview for the metaphor to work, and I meant it. Everything in the last three
chapters stands on its own.

This chapter is where I tell you where it actually came from, and what she believed it
meant.

I'm doing this for two reasons. First, because it's the truth about how I got here, and a
book that hides its sources isn't worth much. Second, because some of you are going to
find what follows enormously useful, and I'm not willing to keep it from you just because
the rest of you might find it strange.

If it's not your thing, skip to Chapter 8. You'll lose nothing. I mean that — there's no
hidden dependency, no callback you'll miss later.

Still here? Good. Let's go.

---

### Who she was

Dolores Cannon spent decades doing hypnosis work — she called it Quantum Healing
Hypnosis Technique. Thousands of sessions, thousands of clients, over something like
forty years. In deep hypnotic states her clients would describe things: past lives, the
period between lives, the reasons they said they'd chosen to be born.

Her whole body of work rests on the reports that came out of those sessions.

Here's what got my attention, and it wasn't the mysticism. It was the **structure of the
evidence**, and it's the same thing that convinced me on a shop floor for thirty years.

I'd been deep in the Seth material for a while — Jane Roberts, *Seth Speaks*, the whole
framework where consciousness comes first and belief runs the code. It resonated. But the
engineer in me kept filing the same objection: *this is one channel. One woman in upstate
New York. Where's the second measurement?*

Cannon was the second measurement. Different method entirely — hypnosis instead of
channeling, thousands of separate strangers instead of one source, decades later, and
none of her clients had read the other ones' transcripts.

And the picture that came out matched. Not vaguely. The same architecture.

In fabrication, when two independent measurements agree, you stop second-guessing and you
cut the steel. That's not proof — I want to be careful here — but it's the thing that made
me stop scrolling and start listening.

---

### What she actually claimed

Fair summary, as best I can give it.

Cannon's central idea was the **Three Waves of Volunteers**. She held that Earth is going
through a transition — a shift to what she called the New Earth, a higher-vibrational
version of reality — and that souls volunteered from elsewhere to incarnate here and help
it along. Three waves, arriving at different times, each with different characteristics
and difficulties.

She connected ADHD directly to the second and third waves.

Her second wave: souls who feel permanently out of place, frustrated by systems that seem
mundane and pointless, unable to make themselves participate in structures they sense are
misaligned with what they came here to do.

Her third wave: children born from roughly the late nineties onward, highly sensitive,
intuitive, wired for hands-on and experiential learning, and constitutionally unable to
tolerate rote memorization.

And the specific claim about us: that people with ADHD aren't deficient in attention but
**processing more at once** — that the distractibility is a side effect of running
multiple streams simultaneously, and the friction with school and office is the friction
between a faster system and a slower environment.

She said that inability to focus on assigned tasks reflects resistance to structures that
don't serve, and that these individuals function as disruptors — that the mismatch is
pointing at something wrong with the system, not with the person.

That's her position, honestly stated. There's more — past lives in non-linear
civilizations, energy work, starseed children — and if you want it, go read *The Three
Waves of Volunteers and the New Earth* and get it from her rather than my summary.

### I didn't just watch a video

Here's the part I left out of Chapter 4.

I didn't stay on the couch. A few months after that 2am video, I went and did it. Twice.
Two QHHT sessions, the first in November of 2024.

If you've never been under, here's what it's actually like: you're not unconscious. You're
lying there, deeply relaxed, and you're talking the entire time — describing out loud
what you're experiencing while a practitioner asks questions. I was narrating to her in
real time.

What I experienced was this. I wasn't in a body. I was a ball of light, and I was
observing a planet. The planet was called Maldek. And I watched it come apart — watched
it implode, and watched the debris tear the atmosphere off Mars.

I'm not going to dress that up or apologize for it. That's what happened in that room and
that's what I said out loud while it was happening. It was as vivid as anything I've
experienced awake, and it did not feel like imagination. It felt like watching.

I walked out of those sessions different. Everything I've built since — the writing, the
tools, this book — traces back through that door. I'd be lying by omission if I left it
out.

---

### What that does and doesn't prove

Now I have to be careful, because I told you in the front matter that I'd only claim what
I can actually stand behind.

**Here's what those two sessions verify.** QHHT is a real practice and I've done it. I
can tell you what the state feels like from the inside rather than from a summary. I had
an experience that was vivid, coherent, emotionally enormous, and permanently changed how
I see things. All of that is firsthand and I'll say it in any room.

**Here's what they don't verify.** Whether what I saw was a memory.

I want to sit in that honestly rather than skate past it, because you deserve better than
a guy who confuses intensity with evidence.

The obvious objection — and it's a good one, and I'd raise it if I were you — is that I'd
been reading Cannon for months before I ever lay down on that table. Maldek is in that
literature. So a fair-minded skeptic says: you filled your head with a cosmology, entered
a highly suggestible state, and produced that cosmology back. That's not a memory
surfacing. That's a mind doing what minds do under hypnosis.

I've thought about that a lot. I don't have a knockdown answer to it. Nobody does, in
either direction — that's the actual state of play with hypnotic testimony, and anyone
who tells you otherwise is selling something.

What I've got is this: I know what it felt like, and it did not feel like invention. I
also know that's exactly what a compelling invention would feel like. Both of those are
true at the same time and I've made my peace with holding them together.

**So here's my position, stated plainly. I believe it.** Based on those sessions and
Cannon's framework, I think I'm a second-wave volunteer, and that belief has shaped how I
live. That's not a hedge — that's what I actually think, and I'm not going to pretend to
be neutral in my own book.

But belief is what I've got. Not proof. And I'm not going to hand you my belief with the
word "verified" stapled to it, because the moment I do that, everything else in this
book — the parts I *can* prove, the parts I tested for twenty years against my own
life — gets dragged down with it.

The mismatch model earns its keep whether or not I was ever a ball of light. That's the
part I'd defend in front of anyone. This part I'll tell you about honestly and let you
decide.

### The part I'm cutting, and why

There's one piece of Cannon's teaching on this that I'm leaving out of my book
deliberately, and I'd rather tell you than have you find it later and wonder if I was
being straight with you.

She argued against medicating people with these traits. Her view was that medication
suppresses the gifts and blocks the purpose. It's stated plainly in her material.

I'm not passing that along.

Not because I've decided she's wrong — I told you in the front matter I don't know enough
about the science to have a position, and I don't. That's exactly why. **Somebody who
doesn't know shouldn't be handing out that advice, and neither should somebody whose
evidence is hypnotic testimony.**

Somebody, somewhere, is reading a book like this one during a bad stretch. If a line of
mine contributes to them stopping something that was holding their life together, that's
on me, and no framework is worth it.

Take everything else from her chapter if it serves you. Leave that part on the shelf. Talk
to a doctor who knows your history.

### Why I kept the chapter

Set the cosmology aside for a second, because there's something underneath it that works
regardless.

Her framework was the first explanation I'd ever encountered that put the problem in the
**pairing** rather than in me. Every other account — clinical, educational, personal —
located the defect inside my skull. Hers put it in the gap between a person and a system.

And once I had that idea, I could test it. Not the metaphysics; the mechanism. Am I
actually worse everywhere, or only in specific rooms? Does this behave like a deficit or
like a mismatch?

Twenty years of checking, and the mismatch model keeps winning. The canopies. The shop
floor. The paperwork I still can't face. That's the part I'll defend anywhere, and I'd
have got to none of it without her.

I could have left Cannon out of this book entirely. Plenty of people would have advised
it. It would be more respectable and it would sell to a wider shelf.

But it would also be a lie by omission, and there's a specific thing she gave me that I
think matters more than whether her cosmology is accurate:

**She was the first person to suggest the mismatch might mean something.**

Every other framing I'd encountered gave me two options — defective, or defective but
coping. She offered a third: that a mind which won't fit an obsolete system might be
information *about the system.*

You don't need volunteer souls for that to be worth something. Any honest look at how we
educate and employ people will tell you our institutions were built for a world that no
longer exists, and that the people who fail hardest inside them are often the ones who
notice first.

You can take that as spiritual or you can take it as sociology. Both readings do the same
work. And a woman with white hair on a 2am video is the reason I ever considered either
one.

That's worth a chapter, and it's worth saying out loud rather than smuggling in.
