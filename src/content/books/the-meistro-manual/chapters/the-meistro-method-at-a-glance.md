# THE MEISTRO METHOD

**Five steps. One continuing investigation.**

You don't need to understand yourself completely to begin. Start with something you've noticed, then follow the evidence.

### 1. OBSERVE
**What actually happened?**

Describe the experience before explaining it.

- What was I doing?
- What did I notice?
- What conditions were present?
- What can I distinguish from memory, assumption, or interpretation?

**Remember:** An experience is evidence that something was experienced. It doesn't automatically establish why it happened.

### 2. QUESTION
**What might explain it?**

Turn the observation into a question rather than a permanent label.

- What are two possible explanations?
- What assumptions am I making?
- What information is missing?
- What would challenge my preferred explanation?

**Remember:** A good question creates possibilities. A premature conclusion closes them.

### 3. CONNECT
**Where else does this appear?**

Look for recurring relationships across situations.

- Have I experienced something similar before?
- What conditions seem to matter?
- When does the pattern *not* appear?
- Am I observing a genuine relationship or simply an interesting resemblance?

**Remember:** Connections suggest hypotheses. They don't prove causes.

### 4. TEST
**What can I safely try?**

Design a small, practical experiment.

- What single condition could I change?
- What outcome would I expect?
- How will I recognise improvement?
- What result would make me reconsider?

**Remember:** Compare what happened with what you predicted. Record the unexpected results too.

### 5. REVISE
**What does the evidence support now?**

Update your understanding without turning it into a permanent identity.

- What have I learned?
- How confident should I be?
- What strategy is worth keeping?
- What remains uncertain?

**Remember:** Changing your mind is not losing progress. It's how understanding improves.

---

## THE INVESTIGATION LOOP

**OBSERVE → QUESTION → CONNECT → TEST → REVISE → OBSERVE AGAIN**

You don't have to complete every step for every experience. Some questions deserve further investigation. Others can remain unanswered.

## THE FIVE RULES

**1. Experience is real. Explanations are testable.**

Respect what you experienced while remaining curious about its causes.

**2. Patterns need exceptions.**

Look for the situations where your explanation doesn't fit.

**3. Conditions matter.**

Investigate the task, environment, tools, timing, and available support before making claims about ability.

**4. A useful strategy is not a permanent identity.**

Keep what works. Reconsider it when circumstances change.

**5. The investigator is still becoming.**

Your current understanding should never become a boundary around your future.
