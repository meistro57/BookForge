# **Chapter 4: From Wishing to Intending**

Whether you’re awake at the wheel or drooling on the deck chair, life is always moving. The question is: are you steering, or are you just hoping the current takes you somewhere nice?

This is the difference between *wishing* and *intending*.

* **Wishing** is throwing a coin in the fountain and hoping something magical happens.

* **Intending** is programming the GPS and actually driving.

The GPS might reroute you, traffic might slow you down, but at least you’ve set a destination. Without it, you’re just idling in a parking lot, wondering why life feels so stagnant.

---

### **The Formula for Intention**

Here’s the simplest version I’ve found:

1. **Name it clearly.** Be specific. “I want connection” is fuzzy. “I want three new friends who get my weird humor” is clear.

2. **Check the parking brake.** Conflicting beliefs kill momentum. If part of you wants connection but another part whispers “people always reject me,” you’ll stay stuck. Naming and challenging those hidden beliefs (remember Chapter 1?) is crucial.

3. **Take inspired action.** Not forced, desperate, or frantic action. Just the next step that feels alive. Text someone. Join a group. Make the first move.

4. **Surrender the outcome.** The hardest part. Do what you can, then let go. Obsession strangles progress.

Think of it like planting a seed. You water it, give it sun, pull weeds—but you don’t stand there screaming “grow faster\!” at the dirt.

---

### **Misfit Mechanics: Intention with an ADHD Brain**

Here’s where most self-help books miss the mark. For neurodivergent minds, intention doesn’t always look like calm focus and steady progress.

* **Scattered attention?** Use it. Set micro-intentions: one-day or one-hour goals. Instead of “I’ll write a book this year,” try “I’ll sketch one messy page this afternoon.”

* **Hyperfocus?** Leverage it. When your brain locks in, ride the wave. Set an intention *before* you dive so your focus has a direction.

* **Forgetfulness?** Externalize. Sticky notes, whiteboards, phone alarms—turn your environment into a cue system that keeps your intention in front of you.

Intentions for misfits aren’t about perfect discipline. They’re about finding rhythms that match your natural wiring.

---

### **The Paradox of Surrender**

Here’s the part that drives people nuts: you have to *let go* of what you want to get it.

Why? Because gripping too tightly signals fear. And fear narrows your field of vision. You miss opportunities that could have been right in front of you.

Surrender doesn’t mean apathy. It means trusting that you’ve done your part—and then creating space for life to surprise you. The “neighbor friendship” example is a perfect case: I didn’t force it, I just stayed open, acted on what felt right, and let the connection grow.

---

### **Action vs. Attachment**

How do you know the difference?

* **Inspired action feels energizing.** Even if it’s hard work, it feels like movement.

* **Attachment feels like panic.** Endless checking, obsessing, refreshing, doubting.

Persistence is good when it’s steady. Attachment is when you start trying to micromanage the universe.

---

### **Timelines and Reality Checks**

Here’s what most manifestation guides leave out: **not everything is in your control.**

* You can’t “intend” your way out of gravity.

* You can’t “intend” someone else to love you back.

* You *can* intend to show up as your best self, to create opportunities, to align your actions with your values.

So when do you pivot? My rule of thumb: if you’ve been consistent for a season (a few months) with no movement at all, it’s time to reassess. Maybe the timing is off. Maybe the intention needs tweaking. Maybe it’s not aligned with what really matters to you.

---

### **Respecting the Skeptics**

Let’s be real: some people gag the moment they hear “manifestation.” That’s fine. Call it psychology.

Athletes visualize their performance. Doctors use placebo effects. Entrepreneurs set intentions before pitching investors. It’s not magic—it’s the science of attention, expectation, and behavior shaping results.

Whether you call it “universal energy” or “neuroplasticity,” the principle is the same: what you focus on expands.

---

### **Troubleshooting Intentions**

What if nothing happens?

* **Check clarity.** Is your intention too vague?

* **Check the parking brake.** Any hidden belief blocking it?

* **Check your actions.** Are you moving at all, or just daydreaming?

* **Check attachment.** Are you suffocating the process with obsession?

If you’ve covered those bases, maybe the answer is simple: it’s not this path, not this timing. Try again. Adjust. Intention isn’t one-and-done—it’s a living experiment.

---

### **The Bottom Line**

Intention isn’t about bending reality to your will. It’s about lining up your mind, emotions, and actions so they point in the same direction—and then letting life meet you halfway.

For misfits, this isn’t about forcing your chaotic brain into a rigid system. It’s about harnessing your unique rhythms—your bursts, your spirals, your wild connections—and pointing them somewhere meaningful.

Set the GPS. Drive. And don’t forget to enjoy the scenery while you’re at it.
