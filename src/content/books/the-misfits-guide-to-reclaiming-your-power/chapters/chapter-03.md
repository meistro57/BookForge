# **Chapter 3: Reality Check – Who’s Driving This Thing?**

Let’s be clear from the start: you are not a Jedi who can levitate your coffee mug just by squinting at it. (If you are, call me—I’ve got a list of IKEA furniture that needs assembling.)

But here’s what *is* true: the way you perceive the world—and the beliefs, assumptions, and filters you run it through—radically changes the world you end up living in. Two people can walk into the same party: one sees awkward strangers and leaves early, the other sees future best friends and stays all night. Same environment, wildly different outcomes.

This isn’t cosmic woo. It’s psychology. Our brains are constantly filtering reality through something called the **Reticular Activating System (RAS)**. Think of it as the bouncer at the nightclub of your mind. It decides which information gets in and which gets tossed to the curb. If you believe “people don’t like me,” your RAS highlights every frown and ignores every smile. If you believe “opportunities are everywhere,” you notice the job posting pinned to the bulletin board or overhear the conversation that leads to your next project.

For misfits and ADHD minds, this filter is both a blessing and a curse. On one hand, our RAS is like a cracked fire hydrant—it lets in way more stimulation than most people’s brains can handle. We notice the flicker of the overhead light, the hum of the fridge, the text buzzing in our pocket, the half-formed idea that just shot across the mental sky. On the other hand, that flood of inputs makes us more likely to notice the hidden connections, the oddball opportunities, the synchronicities that other people miss.

**Here’s the punchline:** you don’t create reality out of thin air, but you *do* create the version of reality you experience. Your brain is basically driving a car with tinted windows—you see the world through the shade you’ve chosen, whether you realized it or not.

So what does this mean for us misfits?

1. **Our attention is a superpower.** Yes, it wanders, it zigzags, it sometimes bolts out the door and comes back three days later with a new tattoo. But where we *choose* to point it determines the kind of reality we’ll live in. ADHD brains are like high-powered flashlights—they can either flicker all over the room or shine like a laser.

2. **Beliefs are steering wheels.** If you believe you’re “bad at finishing things,” guess what? Your RAS will faithfully show you every unfinished project like a Greatest Hits album of shame. Shift the belief, shift the steering.

3. **You can’t control every car on the road, but you can control how you drive.** Life will throw potholes, traffic jams, and the occasional flaming mattress off the overpass. Your job isn’t to magically remove them—it’s to choose how you steer, swerve, or brake.

Now, this is where most books either veer off into mystical territory (“you are the universe manifesting itself\!”) or slam the door with hard-nosed rationalism (“it’s just neurons, deal with it”). We’re not doing either. We’re misfits—we’re taking the middle lane.

Here’s our middle lane:

* Your mind does not control *everything*.

* But it *does* control how you see, what you notice, and how you respond.

* And those responses create ripples that change the actual world you live in.

Think of it like setting the radio station in your car. If you tune to Angry Talk Radio, you’ll drive differently than if you tune to Chill Vibes FM. The outside world hasn’t changed—but your version of it has.

That’s the foundation. This chapter isn’t about conjuring Teslas with your thoughts. It’s about understanding that the reality you experience is deeply shaped by the beliefs and attention habits you carry around.

Which means—spoiler for the next chapter—if you can *consciously* aim those beliefs and attention habits, you can start shaping outcomes in practical, tangible ways. Not by bending the cosmos to your will, but by driving with your hands actually on the wheel.

So before we start talking about “intention” in Chapter 4, check your dashboard:

* What station are you tuned to?

* What’s the story you keep repeating about yourself?

* And is your flashlight-brain pointed at what you want to see—or what you fear?
