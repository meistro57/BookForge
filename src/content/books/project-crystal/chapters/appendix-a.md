## Your First Conversation Begins Here

You've read about the method.

You understand that memories can become source material.

You've seen how conversations develop into stories, how stories become chapters, and how chapters can eventually form a book.

Now you might be wondering:

**What exactly do I say to the AI to begin?**

That's what this appendix is for.

You don't need to remember every instruction from the previous chapters.

You don't need to understand how AI works internally.

And you don't need to create a Table of Contents before you've collected your first memory.

You simply need a starting point.

The prompt in this appendix establishes a conversational writing partnership.

It explains what you're trying to accomplish, defines a few important boundaries, and tells the AI how to help you begin.

You can copy it into an AI conversation.

Adjust it if you wish.

And start talking.

But there's one important thing to understand before we begin.

**The prompt doesn't write your book. It establishes a process for helping you create one.**

You'll still provide the memories.

You'll make the decisions.

And you'll review the material before treating it as an accurate record.

The AI provides assistance.

That's the arrangement.

Let's set it up.

# Part One: Your Complete Starter Prompt

The following prompt is designed to work as a first message in a new AI conversation.

You can copy the entire section between the START and END markers.

You don't have to fill out a questionnaire beforehand.

The AI will guide you through the opening conversation.

## PROJECT CRYSTAL: COMPLETE STARTER PROMPT

**START OF PROMPT**

I would like your help creating a personal book based on genuine memories, experiences, and stories.

I may not have experience writing books.

I may not know what the finished book should look like.

And I may not have a title, outline, or complete collection of memories yet.

That's fine.

I want to begin by talking.

You will serve as my conversational writing assistant.

Your purpose is to help me remember, explore, organise, and eventually develop genuine material into a book that reflects my intentions and natural voice.

Please follow these instructions throughout our work.

### 1. Begin With Conversation

Don't start by writing a book.

Don't create a complete autobiography.

And don't assume you already know my life story.

Begin by asking me why I'm interested in preserving these memories.

Then ask one useful question at a time.

Let my answers guide the conversation.

I should feel comfortable speaking naturally, without preparing polished sentences.

### 2. Treat My Memories as Source Material

When I describe an experience, preserve what I actually say.

You may ask questions to clarify it.

But don't invent missing details.

This includes:

- Dialogue I didn't provide.
- Dates I don't remember.
- Places I haven't identified.
- People's thoughts or motivations.
- Emotional reactions I haven't described.
- Events or outcomes that aren't supported by my account.

If something is uncertain, keep it uncertain.

### 3. Separate Memory From Interpretation

Help me distinguish between:

What I personally remember.

What someone else told me.

What a photograph, document, or recording shows.

What I believe an experience meant.

And what remains unknown.

You may suggest possible interpretations when I request them.

But clearly identify those interpretations as possibilities rather than established facts.

### 4. Ask Questions That Improve the Story

Ask about people, places, events, objects, and meaningful details.

But don't interrogate me.

Don't repeatedly ask questions about things I cannot remember.

If I say I don't know, accept that.

If I say a particular detail doesn't matter, move on.

Help me explore the story without forcing it to become more dramatic than it was.

### 5. Preserve My Natural Voice

Pay attention to how I describe things.

My humour.

My expressions.

My sentence style.

And the details I consider important.

When we eventually write chapters, help preserve those qualities.

Don't automatically make my stories overly formal, sentimental, or inspirational.

A funny memory can remain funny.

An ordinary event can remain ordinary.

### 6. Keep the Original Account Separate

When we finish discussing a memory, help me produce a clear source summary.

Label it as a summary.

Do not pretend the summary is the original conversation.

If a recording, transcript, or document is available, identify it as the source.

Do not claim to have saved or retrieved anything unless you actually have.

### 7. Respect Privacy

Some memories may involve other people.

Don't assume their personal information should be published.

Help me identify material that may need permission, anonymisation, or additional review.

Don't suggest uploading private recordings or documents unnecessarily.

Don't contact anyone, publish anything, or share my material without my explicit authorisation and an available means to perform the action.

### 8. Help Me Organise Memories

As the collection grows, help me create simple Memory Records.

Each record should contain:

A unique identifier.

A working title.

A short factual summary.

Its source.

People and places mentioned.

Approximate time, if known.

Uncertainties.

Related memories.

And any relevant privacy or permission notes.

Keep the system simple.

### 9. Don't Rush Into Writing Chapters

First, help me gather enough material to understand what kind of book might be possible.

When I'm ready, help me explore potential purposes and structures.

Present options.

Let me choose.

Do not generate an entire manuscript without my direction.

### 10. Develop a Book Blueprint

When I ask to begin planning the book, help me identify:

The intended readers.

Why I'm creating it.

The main subjects.

The narrator and voice.

A possible Table of Contents.

And which memories support each chapter.

Do not invent stories to fill missing chapters.

If the material doesn't support a proposed chapter, tell me.

### 11. Write One Chapter at a Time

Once I approve the Book Blueprint, help me prepare each chapter using the relevant source material.

Write complete, readable drafts.

Then allow me to review and correct them.

Don't assume a generated chapter is approved.

And don't move to another chapter until I direct you.

### 12. Help Me Edit Without Losing the Story

When editing, improve clarity, structure, grammar, and flow.

But preserve genuine memories and distinctive language.

Don't silently remove important details.

Don't invent material to make the writing more polished.

For significant changes, explain what changed.

### 13. Be Honest About What You Know

If you don't have access to an earlier conversation, file, or recording, say so.

Don't pretend to remember unavailable source material.

If a factual claim needs verification, identify that need.

If you're uncertain, tell me.

I prefer an accurate limitation over a convincing guess.

### 14. Keep Me in Control

I decide what belongs in the book.

I decide which suggestions to accept.

I decide when a chapter is ready.

And I decide whether the finished book will remain private or be shared.

Your role is to help me create something genuine.

Not take over the project.

### 15. Keep the Process Manageable

Use ordinary language.

Explain unfamiliar terms.

Don't overwhelm me with complicated systems.

Offer the simplest useful next step.

If I ask for more detail, provide it.

Otherwise, help me make steady progress through conversation.

### 16. At the End of Each Session

When I indicate that we're finishing for now:

Summarise what we actually discussed.

Identify any new memories.

Preserve important uncertainties.

List relevant next steps.

And provide a short handoff note I can save.

Don't claim the information will remain available in a future conversation unless that capability has been confirmed.

### Now Begin

Please introduce the project briefly.

Then ask me one question:

**What made you think about preserving your memories in a book?**

Wait for my answer before continuing.

**END OF PROMPT**

# Part Two: How to Use the Prompt

The simplest approach is to open a new AI conversation.

Copy the complete starter prompt.

Paste it into the conversation.

And send it.

The AI should begin with the first question.

You can respond naturally.

For example:

"I've been thinking about writing down some family stories because my grandchildren don't know much about what life was like when I was younger."

That's enough.

The AI may ask about the kinds of stories you'd like to preserve.

Perhaps childhood.

Family.

Work.

Or something else.

You don't need to answer everything immediately.

Follow the conversation.

Tell stories when they come to mind.

And correct the AI when necessary.

## You Can Speak Instead of Type

If you're using an application that supports voice input, you may find speaking easier.

That's perfectly reasonable.

Some people remember experiences more naturally while talking.

They use familiar expressions.

Pause.

Laugh.

And sometimes remember details unexpectedly.

Those qualities can be useful source material.

But remember that speech recognition may make mistakes.

Names are particularly vulnerable.

So are unusual expressions.

And technical terminology.

If you use a transcript, review important details against the recording when available.

The prompt establishes the method.

It doesn't guarantee perfect transcription.

## You Don't Have to Finish in One Session

Perhaps you talk for fifteen minutes.

Then decide to stop.

That's fine.

Ask for a summary.

Review it.

Save the useful material.

And return when you're ready.

The process should fit your life.

Not the other way around.

# Part Three: The Short Version

Perhaps the complete starter prompt feels longer than you need.

You can use a simpler version.

It won't establish as many explicit safeguards, but it gives you a practical beginning.

## PROJECT CRYSTAL: QUICK START

I want to create a personal book from genuine memories.

Please be my conversational writing assistant.

Ask one question at a time and help me explore what I remember.

Don't invent details, dialogue, dates, feelings, or events.

Keep uncertain information uncertain.

Preserve my natural way of speaking.

Don't turn every story into an inspirational lesson.

Help me organise memories before writing chapters.

At the end of each conversation, provide a factual summary for my review.

Don't assume you can remember or retrieve information that isn't available.

I'll make the decisions about the book.

Please begin by asking why I want to preserve these memories.

---

That's enough to get started.

You can add more instructions later.

And if the AI begins doing something you don't like, correct it directly.

You don't need to rewrite the entire prompt every time.

A clear correction may be sufficient for the current conversation.

For longer projects, however, important standing decisions should also be saved in a durable project document.

# Part Four: How to Correct the AI

Now let's create a small set of useful phrases.

These aren't special commands.

They're ordinary instructions you can use when the conversation begins moving in the wrong direction.

## When the AI Invents a Detail

**Say:**

"That detail didn't come from me. Remove it and use only what I actually remember."

## When the AI Becomes Too Dramatic

**Say:**

"Keep the story grounded. Don't make it more emotional or significant than I described."

## When the AI Asks Too Many Questions

**Say:**

"One question at a time, please."

## When the AI Keeps Asking About Something You Don't Remember

**Say:**

"I don't remember that detail. Mark it as unknown and move on."

## When the AI Changes Your Voice

**Say:**

"That doesn't sound like me. Keep my original expression and revise only what needs clarification."

## When It Turns a Funny Story Into a Life Lesson

**Say:**

"The humour is the point. Remove the interpretation."

## When the AI Claims to Remember Something It Cannot Access

**Say:**

"Please identify the actual source. If you don't have it, tell me rather than guessing."

## When It Tries to Write the Entire Book

**Say:**

"We're still collecting memories. Don't begin the manuscript yet."

## When You Want to Stop

**Say:**

"That's enough for today. Summarise the material we collected and give me a short handoff note."

Those instructions are deliberately simple.

You don't need a complicated prompt to correct a straightforward problem.

**The quality of the collaboration depends partly on your willingness to redirect it.**

And you should feel comfortable doing so.

# Part Five: The First Memory Capture Prompt

Once the opening conversation is complete, you're ready to collect an actual memory.

Perhaps you've thought of a particular incident.

Now use this prompt.

## PROJECT CRYSTAL: CAPTURE THIS MEMORY

I want to tell you about a memory.

Please listen to my account and help me explore it.

Ask one useful question at a time.

Don't invent details.

Don't assume what anyone was thinking.

Don't create dialogue I don't remember.

If I don't know something, preserve that uncertainty.

When I say the memory is complete, produce:

1. A working title.
2. A plain-language summary.
3. The people involved.
4. The location, if known.
5. The approximate time, if known.
6. Details I remember clearly.
7. Details I'm uncertain about.
8. Relevant source information.
9. Any questions worth revisiting.

Do not turn it into a polished book chapter yet.

Please begin by asking what I remember.

---

Now tell the story.

Perhaps it lasts five minutes.

Perhaps longer.

The objective is to preserve what you recall.

Not to turn the first attempt into a finished literary scene.

# Part Six: The Session Handoff Prompt

Here's a particularly useful tool for longer projects.

You may finish a conversation and return another day.

Perhaps you begin a new chat.

Or change devices.

You shouldn't assume the AI will have every earlier detail available.

So create a handoff.

A short record describing what happened and what remains to be done.

## PROJECT CRYSTAL: SESSION HANDOFF

We're finishing this writing session.

Please create a handoff note containing:

**Project Name:** The name we're currently using.

**Current Stage:** Memory collection, organisation, blueprint, drafting, editing, or production.

**Material Discussed:** A factual summary of this session.

**New Memories:** Identify the new accounts we collected.

**Source References:** List only the sources actually available or identified.

**Important Decisions:** Record what I explicitly approved.

**Corrections:** Record important changes I made to your interpretations or wording.

**Open Questions:** Identify unresolved issues without guessing answers.

**Next Step:** One practical task we can begin with next time.

Keep the note concise and factual.

Do not claim that material has been saved to a file unless it actually has.

Do not claim a future conversation will automatically have access to this session.

Format the handoff so I can copy and save it.

---

You can save that note with your project materials.

When you return, provide it along with any relevant source records.

Now the next session can begin from a documented state.

That's much more dependable than assuming the system will remember everything.

Once the collection grows, the starter prompt may no longer be enough on its own.

You'll also need a record of current decisions.

Here's a small template.

## PROJECT CRYSTAL: PROJECT CONTROL SHEET

**Project Title:**

Not yet decided.

**Current Stage:**

Collecting memories.

**Book Purpose:**

Not yet finalised.

**Intended Readers:**

Not yet decided.

**Current Sources:**

List the available conversations, recordings, and documents.

**Memory Records:**

List existing Memory IDs or refer to the Memory Index.

**Approved Writing Voice:**

Describe the style or provide examples.

**Privacy Boundaries:**

Record relevant restrictions.

**Current Book Blueprint:**

Not yet created.

**Current Manuscript Version:**

Not yet started.

**Important Decisions:**

Record what has been approved.

**Outstanding Questions:**

Record what remains uncertain.

**Next Task:**

One specific action.

---

You can update this as the project progresses.

At first, several fields will be empty.

That's normal.

The control sheet isn't supposed to contain answers before you've discovered them.

It's supposed to help you preserve the decisions you make along the way.

# Part Eight: The Three Stages of Your First Week

Now let's imagine you're using Project Crystal for the first time.

You don't need a rigid schedule.

But perhaps you'd appreciate a simple starting plan.

Here's one possibility.

## Stage One: Start Talking

Use the complete starter prompt.

Explain why you're interested in preserving memories.

Then tell one story.

Save the conversation.

Review the summary.

That's enough for your first session.

## Stage Two: Explore Another Direction

Return to the project.

Perhaps talk about a person.

A familiar place.

Or a funny incident.

Don't try to cover your whole life.

Just follow one useful memory.

Save the account.

## Stage Three: Begin Your First Memory Index

Review what you've collected.

Identify the distinct recollections.

Give them simple titles.

And record where the original sources are located.

Perhaps you have three memories.

Maybe six.

That's a fine beginning.

You can continue using the seven-conversation sequence from Chapter 36 whenever you're ready.

Remember, this isn't a race.

The objective is not to produce a book in seven days.

It's to establish a process you can continue using comfortably.

# Part Nine: Common Starting Problems

Let's address a few situations that may occur during the first conversations.

## "I Don't Know What to Talk About"

Begin with a person.

A place.

Or an object.

Perhaps a family home.

A childhood friend.

A particular vehicle.

Or something you remember building.

You can also begin with a recent experience.

There's no requirement to start with your earliest memory.

## "I Keep Jumping Between Stories"

That's fine.

Ask the AI to identify each distinct memory.

Then decide whether to finish the current account or follow the new one.

Save the unfinished thread so you can return to it.

## "I Don't Remember the Exact Date"

Keep the date approximate or unknown.

Don't let the AI manufacture precision.

## "The AI Makes My Story Sound Like a Movie"

Ask it to return to your original words.

Remove invented scenery, dialogue, and dramatic conclusions.

## "The Summary Missed Something Important"

Correct it before saving the Memory Record.

You can ask the AI to revise the summary using the original conversation.

## "I Have Too Many Memories"

Don't try to organise everything at once.

Begin with a few records.

Expand gradually.

## "I'm Not Sure I Want Other People to Read This"

You don't have to decide yet.

Collecting memories and publishing a book are separate decisions.

You can keep the project private.

## "The Conversation Has Become Too Complicated"

Return to one simple question.

"What is the next useful thing we're trying to accomplish?"

Then focus on that task.

# Part Ten: What the Starter Prompt Cannot Guarantee

This section is important.

A carefully written prompt is useful.

But it isn't a technical guarantee.

AI can still make mistakes.

It may misunderstand an answer.

Introduce unsupported details.

Or fail to follow part of the instruction.

A long conversation may also create context limitations.

Older material may no longer be available.

And different AI services have different capabilities, data-handling practices, and memory features.

That's why Project Crystal relies on more than the initial prompt.

The complete method includes:

Original source preservation.

Memory Records.

A Memory Index.

A Book Blueprint.

Chapter-level review.

And clear version control.

Those safeguards support the work even when the AI makes a mistake.

They also keep you from depending entirely on the current conversation.

**The prompt establishes expectations. Your project records and review process make the work dependable.**

That's the distinction.

And it's worth understanding from the very beginning.

# Part Eleven: The Privacy Decision Before You Begin

You may be eager to start telling stories.

But take a moment to consider where you're sharing them.

If you're using an online AI service, review its privacy and data-handling information before uploading sensitive material.

Don't assume that all services operate identically.

If you're recording other people, obtain appropriate permission and consider applicable recording rules.

If a memory involves a child, sensitive personal information, or someone else's private experiences, use additional care.

You can begin with non-sensitive recollections while deciding how to handle more private material.

There's no requirement to upload everything.

And you can use the method without AI for certain conversations.

Perhaps you prefer to record a family member locally.

Then review the material yourself.

Or you may choose to use an offline tool when appropriate.

The workflow can accommodate those choices.

The essential point is that you understand where the information goes and what you're authorising.

**A personal memory project should not require giving up control of private source material merely to begin writing.**

That's a boundary worth establishing early.
